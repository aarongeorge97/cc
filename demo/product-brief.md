# Product Brief — FocusForge

## 1. Brand name and what the product does

**FocusForge** is a deep-work timer for indie software developers. It blocks every distraction on your machine — Slack, browser tabs, notifications, even system menus — for the duration of a single 90-minute focus session. When the timer ends, everything comes back. There are no streaks, no badges, no leaderboards. The whole product is one button.

## 2. Key features and competitive differentiators

- **One button.** No setup, no configuration, no goal-setting flow. You open the app, click "Forge," and you're locked in for 90 minutes.
- **Kernel-level distraction blocking.** Unlike browser-extension-based blockers (Cold Turkey, Freedom, Opal), FocusForge runs as a system extension. It can't be bypassed by opening a different browser, switching to mobile, or quitting the app — you literally cannot reach Twitter for 90 minutes.
- **No streaks. No gamification. No notifications.** Streak-based productivity tools (Forest, Streaks, Habitica) create their own anxiety; if you miss a day, you stop using the app entirely. FocusForge doesn't track anything.
- **Shipped to one platform: macOS.** Windows and Linux versions don't exist and aren't promised. The product is built for one machine and one workflow.
- **One-time purchase, $39.** No subscription. No tiers. No "Pro" version.

## 3. Target audience

**Who:** Indie developers, solo founders, and freelance engineers who ship for a living and have spent years trying every productivity tool ever built.

**What they care about:**
- Shipping. Specifically, getting one hard problem solved per day.
- Their own output, not their stats.
- Software that respects their attention — no upsells, no onboarding tutorials, no dashboards.

**What they're frustrated with:**
- Productivity apps that have become engagement products themselves — push notifications about your focus streak, weekly summary emails, "tap to extend your streak" interruptions.
- Tools that make blocking distractions optional ("you can always click 'I really need to' to unblock"). They know they'll click it.
- Subscriptions on tools that should be one-time purchases.
- The slow drift where their best hours of the day get eaten by Slack, Twitter, and "just checking" something.

## 4. Format

**Instagram Reel** — 9:16 vertical, sound-on, designed to land in feeds where the viewer is mid-scroll on their phone in the evening (the moment they're most aware of having wasted their day).

## 5. Target length

**30 seconds, exactly 75 words total** (hook + body + CTA combined). Aggressive — every word has to earn its place. The compact format is intentional: indie devs don't watch long-form ads, and a tight script forces focus on the single sharpest argument.
