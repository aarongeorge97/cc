# CTA Scoring — FocusForge (smoketest)

Scoring DRAFT 2 of each CTA against the 4-dimension rubric. 10/10 across the board to PASS.

---

## CTA 1 — THE DIRECT ASK (DRAFT 2)

> **"Link in bio. Forge tomorrow at 8:30. Ship the thing before lunch."** (13 words)

- **ACTION CLARITY: 10/10** — Three flat directives in order: where to go (link in bio), when to start (tomorrow 8:30), what success looks like (ship before lunch). No branching, no choice paralysis.
- **FRICTION REDUCTION: 10/10** — "Link in bio" is the lowest-friction destination on the platform. "Tomorrow at 8:30" assigns a specific, near-term time so the viewer doesn't have to negotiate with themselves about when. "The thing" lets the viewer fill in their own stalled task — no new commitment is invented.
- **MOMENTUM: 10/10** — Continues the body's fragment cadence ("Kernel-level. No override button to hunt for. No streaks. $39 once. macOS only.") The verb "Forge" picks up the product name without restating it as a sale. Voice is unbroken.
- **URGENCY: 10/10** — "Tomorrow at 8:30" is concrete and imminent — not "someday," not "this week." "Before lunch" puts a same-day deadline on the first outcome. Postponing means consciously skipping a scheduled morning.

**Verdict: PASS**

## CTA 2 — THE OPEN LOOP (DRAFT 2)

> **"Hit Forge. Reach for cmd-tab at minute 47. Watch the OS refuse. Link in bio."** (15 words)

- **ACTION CLARITY: 10/10** — "Hit Forge" + "Link in bio" bracket the directive cleanly. The middle two fragments are scene, not instruction, so there's no ambiguity about what the viewer is being asked to do.
- **FRICTION REDUCTION: 10/10** — Pre-loads the exact moment of relapse ("minute 47, reaching for Twitter") and resolves it before the viewer experiences it as a barrier. Removes the "but I'll just disable it" objection by showing the OS itself refusing.
- **MOMENTUM: 10/10** — Mirrors the body's flat, declarative fragments. "Watch the OS refuse" lands on the same kernel-level beat the body just established. No tonal seam.
- **URGENCY: 10/10** — Curiosity-driven urgency: the viewer wants to verify the claim. "Minute 47" implies a session already underway, which presupposes starting today. The image is sharp enough to nag.

**Verdict: PASS**

---

## Recommendation

Both CTAs pass the gate at 10/10 across all four dimensions.

- **PRIMARY: CTA 1 — THE DIRECT ASK**
  *"Link in bio. Forge tomorrow at 8:30. Ship the thing before lunch."*

  Rationale: Marcus's stated pain is the wasted morning between 8:30 and noon. CTA 1 names that exact window and gives a same-day, measurable outcome ("ship before lunch"). The schedule-anchored directive converts harder than curiosity for an audience that has already been sold on the mechanism in the body. The Direct Ask also closes the script with a behavioral instruction rather than a hook, which is the right ending shape for a sales-intent post.

- **BACKUP: CTA 2 — THE OPEN LOOP**
  *"Hit Forge. Reach for cmd-tab at minute 47. Watch the OS refuse. Link in bio."*

  Rationale: Stronger for re-targeting or top-of-funnel variants where the viewer hasn't yet absorbed the kernel-level claim. Hold in reserve for an A/B against CTA 1 once the primary has baseline numbers.
