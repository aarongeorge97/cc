## Hook 1 — THE BOLD CLAIM (FAIL, needs rewrite)

**Current Draft 3:** 14 focus apps in 6 years. Bypassed every one inside five minutes. The one that held: kernel-level. No override.

**What needs to change:**

1. **Reorder so the bypass lands first, not the count.** "14 in 6 years" is generic ad math. The thumb-stopping fact is "bypassed every one in under five minutes." Lead with that. The count becomes context, not the opener.
2. **Drop "held" entirely.** It is metaphorical and soft. The brand voice does not personify tools. Replace with a flat operational verb or no verb at all (a fragment).
3. **Drop the colon.** Brand voice is period-stops, not colon-reveals. Compare the Voice Guide's "It's raining. The forecast said it wouldn't. The forecast is a productivity app." — no colons, no setups.
4. **Keep "No override."** That is the load-bearing phrase and the closest thing to a Language Bank line in the hook.

**Direction:** Open with the bypass as a flat boast. State the count as a credential, not a setup. Land on "kernel-level. No override." as two separate beats. Aim for 4-5 fragments, no compound sentences, no colon.

**Constraints reminder:** under 25 words, no exclamation points, no "just," no hype words, declarative only, technical terms unexplained.

## Hook 2 — THE PAIN CALL-OUT (FAIL, needs rewrite — closest to passing)

**Current Draft 3:** Your productivity app is the distraction now. Forest emails about your dead streak. Opal pings your focus score. Streaks summarises your week.

**What needs to change:**

1. **The hook tapers instead of cresting.** Four parallel beats (one diagnosis + three examples) produce four small nods, not one involuntary reaction. The structure needs a turn after the proof — a closing beat that re-indicts, not a third example.
2. **Cut to two competitor proofs, not three.** Two examples are proof; three is a list and lists flatten emotional charge. Forest + Opal carries the indictment; Streaks is redundant and the fourth beat is where the hook should turn.
3. **Add a closing indictment that reframes.** Something flat and short that names what is actually happening — e.g., a one-fragment line that says these apps are engagement products, or that the viewer is the one being farmed. Do not write "That is the distraction" (already diagnosed and rejected in Draft 2). Find a different angle: the viewer's role, the category's incentive, the inversion.
4. **Keep "Your productivity app is the distraction now." verbatim.** It is the Language Bank line and it is the strongest sentence in the pool.

**Direction:** Diagnosis + two named-competitor proofs + one closing indictment that turns. Four beats, not four parallel beats. The fourth beat must do different work than the second and third.

**Constraints reminder:** under 25 words, no exclamation points, no "just," no hype words, declarative only, technical terms unexplained.

## Hook 3 — THE CONTRARIAN (FAIL, needs rewrite)

**Current Draft 3:** Streaks are not productivity. They are anxiety with a leaderboard. A focus tool that needs your engagement is not a focus tool.

**What needs to change:**

1. **Sentence three is abstract principle, not concrete.** "A focus tool that needs your engagement" has no object — no app, no feature, no notification, no number. Specificity drops to 8. Replace with a concrete artifact (a dashboard, a streak counter, a weekly email, a focus score) or a Language Bank phrasing.
2. **The hook peaks at sentence two and tapers.** "Anxiety with a leaderboard" is the share-worthy line per the Audience Profile. Sentence three is reasoned argument that follows the punch and dilutes it. Either cut sentence three, or make sentence three a concrete demonstration that re-charges the line rather than explaining it.
3. **Consider ending on the share-worthy line.** Two-sentence hooks are allowed. If the third sentence cannot raise the emotional charge above sentence two, it should not be there. The Voice Guide explicitly favors restraint — "If a word isn't load-bearing, it's gone."

**Direction:** Either (a) cut to two sentences and end on "anxiety with a leaderboard," using the saved word budget for a third beat that is concrete and harder, or (b) replace sentence three with a flat one-fragment artifact callout (a named feature of a competitor, or a feature negation FocusForge ships).

**Constraints reminder:** under 25 words, no exclamation points, no "just," no hype words, declarative only, technical terms unexplained.
