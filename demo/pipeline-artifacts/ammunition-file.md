# Ammunition File — FocusForge (smoke-test stub)

> Stub Ammunition File hand-written for smoke-testing the pipeline. In a real run, this would be produced by `phase1-research-synthesizer` from the YouTube/Reddit/X researcher outputs.

## 1. TOP PAIN POINTS (ranked by emotional intensity)

1. **"My productivity app became the distraction."** Indie devs report that streak-based productivity apps (Forest, Streaks) generate their own anxiety — push notifications about losing your streak, weekly summary emails, daily check-ins. The thing that was supposed to help is now another thing demanding attention.
2. **The "I really need to" bypass.** Every blocker has an unblock button. Devs know they'll click it. They've tested every one — Cold Turkey, Freedom, Opal, BlockSite — and bypassed them all.
3. **"My best 4 hours got eaten by Slack again."** The slow drift where the morning — the only time they have for real engineering work — gets consumed by reactive messages and "just checking" Twitter.
4. **Subscription fatigue on tools that should be one-time purchases.** $7/month for a focus timer feels like a tax on something that should cost a one-off $30.
5. **Productivity gamification is patronizing.** "Earn a badge for focusing!" feels like a dashboard built for a child, not an adult shipping production code.

## 3. PROVEN HOOKS

1. **Bold claim — number-led.** "I've tried 14 productivity apps in 6 years. The one that actually works has one button."
2. **Pain call-out.** "Your productivity app is the distraction now."
3. **Contrarian — attack a category convention.** "Streaks are not productivity. They're anxiety with a leaderboard."
4. **Story opener.** "It's 11 AM. You've answered 23 Slack messages. You've shipped zero code. Sound familiar?"
5. **Identity statement.** "If you're an indie dev still using Forest in 2026, this is for you."
6. **Inversion.** "The best focus app should not have a dashboard."
7. **Unexpected specifics.** "90 minutes. One button. No streaks. $39 once."
8. **Explicit competitor mention.** "Cold Turkey. Freedom. Opal. You've bypassed them all."
9. **Emotional admission.** "I shipped more in college on a $200 laptop than I do now."
10. **Contrarian principle.** "Productivity is the absence of distractions, not the presence of features."

## 4. CONTENT NERVES

1. The streak-is-anxiety nerve — devs love being told it's not their fault for "failing" with Forest/Streaks.
2. The kernel-level vs. browser-extension distinction — feels like a real engineering claim, not marketing.
3. The "subscriptions on simple tools should be illegal" hot take — drives engagement on X.
4. The macOS-only hard stance — controversial, signals discipline.
5. The anti-gamification stance — separates "tools for adults" from "tools for kids."

## 5. COMPETITIVE GAPS

1. **No competitor brags about being un-bypassable.** They all advertise "you can choose your own block list." That's the opposite of what the customer wants.
2. **No competitor positions against productivity gamification.** Even Forest, the OG, leans harder into trees and badges.
3. **No competitor publicly says "we're macOS-only and we will never make a Windows version."** It's a positive — it signals build quality and lack of dilution.
