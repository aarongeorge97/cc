## CTA 1 — THE DIRECT ASK

DRAFT 1: Link in bio. Buy it once. Forge tomorrow at 8:30. Ship one hard problem before lunch.
DIAGNOSIS: 16 words. Cadence is right and "Forge tomorrow at 8:30" addresses Marcus's exact morning. But "Buy it once" leans slightly salesy and is redundant with the body's "$39 once" — feels like reselling something already stated. The promise "Ship one hard problem before lunch" is strong but borders on aspirational; brand voice forbids pep-talk register. Tighten by removing the buy verb and trusting the link.
DRAFT 2: Link in bio. Forge tomorrow at 8:30. Ship the thing before lunch. (13 words)

## CTA 2 — THE OPEN LOOP

DRAFT 1: Try cmd-tab to Twitter mid-session. Nothing happens. Link in bio.
DIAGNOSIS: 11 words. The open loop works — it dangles the exact moment of relief from the audience profile (the half-second of "oh, it's actually impossible"). But "Try cmd-tab to Twitter mid-session" is slightly muddled as a directive; reads as instruction rather than image. Flatten it into a stated outcome so the curiosity is about the experience, not the test. Keep the OS-refusing beat — that's the hook of the loop.
DRAFT 2: Hit Forge. Reach for cmd-tab at minute 47. Watch the OS refuse. Link in bio. (15 words)
