**10/10 rewrite:**

(If even the dash feels off-brand, harder version: "Hit Forge. Slack gone. Twitter gone. Every tab gone. 90 minutes.")

## Three weakest lines

1. **Line 10 — "Link in bio." (7).** Generic platform-CTA. Doesn't sound like this brand specifically.
2. **Line 4 — "Hit Forge — Slack, Twitter, every tab is unreachable for 90 minutes." (8).** Em-dash aside and passive construction soften an otherwise strong line.
3. **Line 6 — "No override button to hunt for." (8).** Over-described refusal. The on-brand version is two words.

## Three strongest lines

1. **Line 1 — "11 AM. 23 Slack replies. Zero commits. You reach for cmd-tab. Your best four hours are already gone."** Best opener possible for this voice. Numbers do the work.
2. **Line 2 — "Your productivity app is the distraction now."** The diagnosis. Listed in the brand voice's canonical phrases for a reason.
3. **Line 12 — "Ship the thing before lunch."** Cold close. Peer register. No sign-off.

## Patterns of weakness

- **Punctuation slips that flatten the cadence.** The brand voice is built on periods between fragments ("$39. Once."). Two of the three weak lines miss a period or substitute an em-dash, which is the difference between a `git log` of declarations and flowing prose. Cheap to fix.
- **Mild over-description on refusals.** "No override button to hunt for" should be "No override." The voice's refusals are bare; any qualifier dilutes them.
- **One passive verb** ("is unreachable") in an otherwise active script. Active voice is the default for this engineer.
- **One generic platform-CTA** ("Link in bio") that any account would say. The rest of the script earns the right to sound like this specific founder; that one line gives it back.

No deeper voice problems — no hype, no aspiration, no warmth-as-tactic, no banned vocabulary. The fixes are surface-level.
