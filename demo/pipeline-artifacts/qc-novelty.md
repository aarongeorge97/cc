# QC — Invention Novelty (FocusForge / smoketest)

Scoring rubric: 1 = actively hurts, 4 = generic filler, 7 = true but generic, 10 = category-defining.

---

## Per-line scores

### Line 1: "11 AM."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: Strong cold-open time-stamp, but a time-of-day opener is a known indie-dev / hustle-content trope. Effective, not unprecedented.
- Rewrite direction: Pin the timestamp to a FocusForge-specific frame (e.g. "11 AM. The session you didn't start.") so the time itself indicts the viewer rather than just sets a scene.

### Line 2: "23 Slack replies."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: The "Slack replies as proxy for wasted morning" image is sharp and audience-specific to indie devs, but the noun phrase itself is a stat any productivity ad could cite.
- Rewrite direction: Specify a tell that only this audience recognizes — e.g. "23 Slack replies. None of them yours to send." — to name the trap, not just count it.

### Line 3: "Zero commits."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining: turns a developer-native metric (git commits) into the moral verdict on the morning. No general-audience productivity ad could say this. Pairs the brag-stat of the audience against itself.

### Line 4: "You reach for cmd-tab."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining: names the exact muscle-memory gesture of a macOS developer mid-distraction. Cold Turkey, Freedom, Opal cannot say "cmd-tab" without sounding off-platform. This line is unsayable by a competitor without breaking their own positioning.

### Line 5: "Your best four hours are already gone."
**Score: 7**
- Why not 10: True and stings, but "your best hours are gone" is a staple line in every focus / productivity ad since Cal Newport. No FocusForge-specific framing.
- Rewrite direction: Replace with something that names *what* those hours were supposed to produce in dev terms — e.g. "The hard problem of the day is still open in tab 14." — so the loss is concrete to a shipper, not abstract.

### Line 6: "Your productivity app is the distraction now."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining indictment of the entire competitive set (Forest, Streaks, Habitica, even Notion). This reframes the category, not the product — which is exactly what a 10-scoring line does. Protect.

### Line 7: "FocusForge is one button."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: "One button" is the actual product truth and feels mechanical-spec rare in this category, but the phrasing "X is one button" is a known minimalist-product trope (Tile, Dropbox-era pitches).
- Rewrite direction: Make the button itself the subject — e.g. "One button. That's the whole app." — to push the claim from feature-description to product-philosophy.

### Line 8: "Hit Forge — Slack, Twitter, every tab is unreachable for 90 minutes."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: Naming the verb ("Forge") as imperative is brand-defining and the word "unreachable" is stronger than the usual "blocked." But the "Slack, Twitter, every tab" enumeration is the standard distraction-blocker list — Cold Turkey would write the same.
- Rewrite direction: Replace the generic enumeration with one specific, unreachable-in-a-way-that-stings target ("Slack is gone. The unread badge stays.") so the claim attaches to FocusForge's kernel-level mechanism, not to the category.

### Line 9: "Kernel-level."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining. Two words that no browser-extension competitor (Cold Turkey, Freedom, Opal) can honestly say. Names the moat in the language the audience respects. Protect.

### Line 10: "No override button to hunt for."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining. Directly indicts the "I really need to unblock this" escape hatch every competitor ships. The phrase "to hunt for" reveals the actual user behavior FocusForge refuses to enable. Unsayable by Freedom / Opal without self-contradiction.

### Line 11: "No streaks."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: Real differentiator vs Forest/Streaks/Habitica, and the brief calls it out explicitly. But "no streaks" alone is now a known anti-gamification pose (e.g. some meditation apps claim it).
- Rewrite direction: Tie the absence to the audience's lived experience — "No streak to break. Nothing to come back to." — so it indicts gamification rather than just opting out.

### Line 12: "$39 once."
**Score: 9**
- Why not 10: Sharp, audience-respecting price tag in a subscription-saturated category. But "$X once" / "no subscription" is now a recognized indie-product flex (Things 3, Tot, many Setapp-adjacent tools).
- Rewrite direction: Frame the price as a refusal, not a number — e.g. "$39. The last time you'll pay for it." — so it argues against the category's economics rather than just stating yours.

### Line 13: "macOS only."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining stance. In a market where every productivity tool brags cross-platform, declaring single-platform as a feature is the kind of frame that signals genuine point of view. Pairs perfectly with the indie-dev audience.

### Line 14: "Link in bio."
**Score: 4**
- Generic filler. Identical to every Reel CTA on Instagram. Says nothing about FocusForge.
- Rewrite direction: Replace with a CTA that uses the product's verb — "Forge link in bio." — or cut and roll the URL into Line 15.

### Line 15: "Forge tomorrow at 8:30."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining. Uses the brand name as a verb ("Forge"), prescribes a specific time, and treats the viewer as someone who ships in the morning. No competitor's CTA reads this way. Protect.

### Line 16: "Ship the thing before lunch."
**Score: 10**
- Category-defining close. "Ship" is the audience's native verb; "the thing" is how indie devs actually refer to their work; "before lunch" stakes the 90-minute claim against a concrete deadline. No general-productivity ad would close this way.
