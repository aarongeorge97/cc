## Hook 1 — THE BOLD CLAIM

DRAFT 1: I tried 14 productivity apps in 6 years. The one that actually works has one button and zero notifications.

DIAGNOSIS 1: The "14 in 6 years" frame is generic ad math and the second clause is two features stapled together. "Actually works" is hedge-language pretending to be punchy. Voice is too close to a testimonial. Needs a sharper number, a sharper refusal.

DRAFT 2: 14 focus apps in 6 years. Bypassed all of them in under five minutes. The one that held me runs at the kernel.

DIAGNOSIS 2: Stronger — "bypassed all of them in under five minutes" pulls Language Bank phrasing and lands the objection. "Held me" is slightly soft and slightly metaphorical. "Runs at the kernel" is the right register but the sentence is three beats when it should be three commits.

DRAFT 3: 14 focus apps in 6 years. Bypassed every one inside five minutes. The one that held: kernel-level. No override.

Word count: 23.

## Hook 2 — THE PAIN CALL-OUT

DRAFT 1: Your productivity app is the distraction now. Another app screaming at you about the thing you were supposed to be working on.

DIAGNOSIS 1: First sentence is the trigger line and lands. Second sentence quotes the Language Bank well but reads as a paraphrase rather than a hit. Two sentences that say the same thing. The pain call-out should be one clean indictment, not a setup-and-confirm.

DRAFT 2: Your productivity app is the distraction now. Forest emails you about your dead streak. Opal pings your focus score. That is the distraction.

DIAGNOSIS 2: Specific competitors land. Ending repeats the opening, which weakens both. "That is the distraction" is filler. The competitor list is the proof — let it close the hook.

DRAFT 3: Your productivity app is the distraction now. Forest emails about your dead streak. Opal pings your focus score. Streaks summarises your week.

Word count: 22.

## Hook 3 — THE CONTRARIAN

DRAFT 1: Streaks are not productivity. They are anxiety with a leaderboard. If your focus app has a dashboard, it is the problem.

DIAGNOSIS 1: First two sentences are the shareable line and they work. Third sentence dilutes by adding a second contrarian claim. Two attacks in one hook splits the punch. Pick the sharper one and stop.

DRAFT 2: Streaks are not productivity. They are anxiety with a leaderboard. The best focus tool has no dashboard, no badge, no tree.

DIAGNOSIS 2: Tighter, but the trailing list ("no dashboard, no badge, no tree") leans on Forest joke too much and softens the contrarian punch. The principle is stronger when it is not followed by a demonstration. Cut to the bone.

DRAFT 3: Streaks are not productivity. They are anxiety with a leaderboard. A focus tool that needs your engagement is not a focus tool.

Word count: 22.

## Hook 4 — THE STORY OPENER

DRAFT 1: It is 11 AM. You have answered 23 Slack messages. You have shipped zero code. You open Twitter to take a break.

DIAGNOSIS 1: Solid opener — pulls directly from the Audience Profile day-in-the-life. "Take a break" is weak as a closer; it is the cliche the audience would tune out. Needs a turn that raises the question the viewer must answer.

DRAFT 2: 11 AM. 23 Slack replies. Zero commits. You reach for cmd-tab to check Twitter. Tomorrow morning will look exactly the same.

DIAGNOSIS 2: Fragments hit harder. "Zero commits" is more on-voice than "zero code." The cmd-tab detail is correct technical register. Last line is the indictment but lands flat — "exactly the same" is a half-resolution. The hook should leave the viewer mid-recognition, not at a verdict.

DRAFT 3: 11 AM. 23 Slack replies. Zero commits. You reach for cmd-tab. Your best four hours are already gone.

Word count: 19.
